# Healthcare Utilisation & Cost Analysis | Power BI

##  Project Overview

This project analyses healthcare utilisation and financial performance using Power BI. It explores billing amounts, medication costs, treatment costs, insurance coverage, out-of-pocket expenses, room charges, medical procedures, diagnoses, and departmental costs.

The dashboard was designed to transform healthcare data into interactive visual insights that can help stakeholders explore financial patterns, healthcare utilisation, and differences across departments and procedures.

##  Key Areas of Analysis

### 1. Financial Overview

Examines billing amounts and the distribution of major healthcare costs, including medication, treatment, insurance coverage, out-of-pocket expenses, and room charges.

### 2. Medical Procedure Analysis

Compares billing amounts across medical procedures to explore differences in the financial values associated with each procedure category.

### 3. Diagnosis and Service Type Analysis

Explores billing amounts across recorded diagnoses and service types, allowing comparisons between emergency, inpatient, and outpatient services.

### 4. Departmental Cost Analysis

Examines healthcare costs across departments to identify differences in recorded financial activity.

### 5. Geographic Analysis

Uses location-based visualisations to explore how recorded billing amounts vary across cities and states.

## Key Findings

Initial analysis of 5,000 healthcare visits identified the following patterns:

- Treatment costs totalled 2,630,406, compared with 546,039 in medication costs.
- Of the 5,000 visits, 3,085 were marked Paid and 1,915 were marked Pending.
- Pending visits represented 38.3% of the recorded visits.
- Provider 5 recorded the highest visit volume and total treatment costs among the five provider IDs. It also had the lowest average patient satisfaction score in the initial analysis.
- Cardiology recorded the highest total treatment costs among the five departments.
- Missing insurance coverage and incomplete admission, discharge, follow-up, and room-type information were identified as data quality considerations.

Note: Financial values are presented in the dataset's recorded units because the currency has not been confirmed. These findings are descriptive and do not establish causation or independently demonstrate provider efficiency.

## Data Limitations

- The currency of the financial fields has not been established.
- Missing values may affect some calculations.
- Provider costs and patient satisfaction alone do not establish clinical quality or efficiency.
- Pending payments require further investigation before being interpreted as outstanding financial liabilities.
- Observed trends require validation before being attributed to operational changes.

## Data Quality and Limitations

The analysis is based on the healthcare dataset supplied for this project.

The results describe the records available in the dataset and should not automatically be interpreted as representative of real-world healthcare providers.

Billing amounts and recorded costs do not necessarily represent profit, net revenue, or overall financial sustainability.

Departmental billing differences should not be interpreted as definitive measures of provider efficiency or quality without additional information about workload, staffing, case complexity, and patient outcomes.

The source and provenance of the dataset should be verified before making claims about its representativeness or real-world applicability.
